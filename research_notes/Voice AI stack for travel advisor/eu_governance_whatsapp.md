## Q1. EU AI Act: which obligations apply to a consumer-facing conversational AI agent, and when do they bite?

### Takeaway
For a consumer-facing voice agent, the binding AI Act hooks are **Article 50 transparency** (disclose the user is talking to a machine; mark synthetic audio machine-readably), applying from **2 August 2026 — this date was *not* postponed by the Digital Omnibus** — plus the **Article 5 prohibitions already in force since 2 February 2025**. The high-risk regime (Annex III / Annex I) was pushed out to **2 December 2027 / 2 August 2028** by the Digital Omnibus on AI, which sources report entered into force **27 July 2026**. A travel-advice voice agent is very unlikely to be Annex III high-risk, so the practical live obligation set today is Article 50 + Article 5 + whatever flows down from the GPAI model providers in the stack.

### Cited Findings

**Article 50 — transparency (the core binding obligation)**
- Article 50(1): providers must design AI systems that **interact directly with natural persons** — explicitly "chatbots, AI agents, and avatars" — so that people are informed they are interacting with AI; the information must be "clear, distinguishable and accessible and provided no later than the first interaction or exposure." — [European Commission, Shaping Europe's digital future — FAQ: Transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) (search-result surface, read 19 Sep 2026); [EC AI Act Service Desk — Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50)
- Article 50(2): providers of AI systems, **including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio**, image, video or text "shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated" — i.e. watermarks or metadata a machine can read. — [EC FAQ, Article 50](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [nordflux — EU AI Act Article 50: Labelling Obligation for Chatbots and Voice Agents from 2 August 2026](https://nordflux.de/en/insights/eu-ai-act-article-50-labelling-obligation-for-chatbots-and-voice-agents-from-2-august-2026)
- Article 50 obligations **apply from 2 August 2026** and expressly cover "voice AI, synthetic audio, deepfakes, and public-interest text." — [nordflux](https://nordflux.de/en/insights/eu-ai-act-article-50-labelling-obligation-for-chatbots-and-voice-agents-from-2-august-2026); [hard2bit — AI Act Article 50: AI transparency from 2 August 2026](https://hard2bit.com/en/blog/ai-act-article-50-ai-transparency-chatbots-deepfakes/)
- **Article 50 was NOT delayed by the Digital Omnibus.** "While the Digital Omnibus pushed high-risk AI deadlines into 2027 and 2028, the Article 50 transparency obligations were left untouched and still apply from 2 August 2026." — [Goodwin — Not Delayed, Not Deferred: EU AI Act Transparency Obligations Are Now in Force (Aug 2026)](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force) (title/summary via search; full text egress-blocked); [aiactblog.nl — Article 50 transparency obligations: the AI Act deadline of 2 August 2026 that has not been postponed](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/article-50-transparency-deadline-2-august-2026)
- **One narrow carve-out reported:** the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking/watermarking duty is reported to apply from **2 December 2026 instead of 2 August 2026 — a four-month extension — but only for systems already placed on the market as at 2 August 2026.** `[UNVERIFIED — secondary source only; this is a materially load-bearing detail and must be checked against the Omnibus text in the OJ]` — [Usercentrics — EU AI Act Deal: Digital Omnibus Now in Force](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)

**Digital Omnibus on AI — the 2026 delay/simplification package**
- Sequence reported: European Parliament approved the Digital Omnibus on AI on **16 June 2026**; Council final adoption **29 June 2026**; **entered into force 27 July 2026**. `[UNVERIFIED — secondary source only]` — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)
- New high-risk deadlines: **Annex III high-risk → 2 December 2027**; **Annex I (product-embedded) high-risk → 2 August 2028**. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); consistent framing in [Gibson Dunn — EU AI Act Omnibus Agreement: Postponed High-Risk Deadlines and Other Key Changes](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (search surface only; fetch blocked) and [Cloud Security Alliance — EU AI Act's High-Risk Deadline: Deferred, Not Cancelled](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/)
- The deferral is characterised across sources as a pragmatic pause to let standards and guidance catch up, **not** a repeal — standards were still delayed and guidance still pending as of Aug 2026. — [Plesner — AI Act August 2026: what to expect — delayed standards, pending guidance, and the Digital Omnibus on AI](https://plesner.com/en/news/ai-act-august-2026-what-expect-delayed-standards-pending-guidance-and-digital-omnibus-ai)

**Article 5 — prohibited practices (already in force)**
- **Chapters I and II of the AI Act (which include Article 5) became applicable on 2 February 2025.** — [Wolters Kluwer Global Workplace Law & Policy — The Prohibition of AI Emotion Recognition Technologies in the Workplace under the AI Act](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/)
- **Article 5(1)(f)** prohibits placing on the market, putting into service for that purpose, or use of AI systems **to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions**, except for medical or safety reasons. — [Wolters Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/); [FPF — Red Lines under EU AI Act: emotion recognition in the workplace and education](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/)
- Scope limit that matters for a consumer travel agent: the prohibition bites only on systems inferring emotions **from biometric data** (facial expression, voice tone, physiological signals); systems detecting sentiment **from written text** are outside it. — [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [Bundesnetzagentur — Prohibited practices](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/EN/Areas/Digitalisation/AI/08_ProhibitedPractices/start.html)
- Penalties for breach of Article 5: up to **€35 million or 7% of global annual turnover**, whichever is higher. — [teamed. — Is Emotion Recognition at Work Legal in EU? No (2026)](https://www.teamed.global/insights/is-emotion-recognition-at-work-legal-in-eu)
- The Commission approved **non-binding guidelines on prohibited AI practices on 4 February 2025**; a consolidated Commission guidelines document exists as `C(2025) 5052 final`, Brussels **29.7.2025**. — [EC AI Act Service Desk — Guidelines on prohibited AI practices (PDF, C(2025) 5052 final, 29.7.2025)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-08/guidelines_on_prohibited_artificial_intelligence_practices_established_by_regulation_eu_20241689_ai_act_english_ied3r5nwo50xggpcfmwckm3nuc_112367-1.PDF); [Wolters Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/)

**GPAI (general-purpose AI model) obligations**
- **From 2 August 2025**, GPAI obligations apply to **newly placed** GPAI models. — [MediaLaws — EU AI Obligations for GPAI Providers: Compliance, Enforcement & Deadlines (2025–2027)](https://www.medialaws.eu/eu-ai-obligations-for-gpai-providers-compliance-enforcement-deadlines-2025-2027/); [Mayer Brown — Rules on General-Purpose AI Start Applying (Aug 2025)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/eu-ai-act-news-rules-on-general-purpose-ai-start-applying-guidelines-and-template-for-summary-of-training-data-finalized)
- **The Commission may begin enforcing GPAI obligations from 2 August 2026.** Models already on the EU market as at 2 August 2025 must be brought into compliance by **2 August 2027**. — [MediaLaws](https://www.medialaws.eu/eu-ai-obligations-for-gpai-providers-compliance-enforcement-deadlines-2025-2027/); [Arnold & Porter — Does Your Company Have EU AI Act Compliance Obligations as a GPAI Model Provider?](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/08/does-your-company-have-eu-ai-act-compliance-obligations)
- The **GPAI Code of Practice** is formally voluntary but functions as the compliance bridge; a **training-data summary template** was finalised alongside the Aug 2025 guidelines. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/eu-ai-act-news-rules-on-general-purpose-ai-start-applying-guidelines-and-template-for-summary-of-training-data-finalized); [EU AI Act — Introduction to the Code of Practice](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/introduction-to-code-of-practice/)

### Inferences
- A travel-advice voice agent is almost certainly **not** an Annex III high-risk system (it is not employment, credit, education, law enforcement, migration or essential-services eligibility), so the December-2027 deferral is **irrelevant to the build** — the team should not treat "high-risk delayed" as breathing room. The Article 50 clock already ran out on 2 August 2026.
- Building the product as a **deployer of third-party GPAI models** (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google) rather than as a GPAI provider keeps the GPAI obligations upstream — but only as long as the team does not fine-tune in a way that could make it a downstream provider. Sources did not settle that threshold; the Commission's GPAI guidelines are the place to look.
- Emotion inference is a **live trap even for a consumer product**: an agent that infers frustration/anger from *voice tone* is doing biometric-based emotion inference. Article 5(1)(f) is workplace/education-scoped, so consumer travel advice is outside that prohibition — but emotion recognition outside the prohibition still triggers the Article 50(3) notification duty (see gaps) and is a high-risk-category candidate under Annex III in other framings.

### Gaps
- **I could not read Article 50 paragraphs (3)–(7) in the primary text.** In particular I could not verify the exact wording of the **Article 50(3) duty on deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems to inform exposed persons**, nor the **Article 50(4) deep-fake disclosure duty**, nor the **Article 50(5)** requirement that information be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" in a clear and distinguishable manner, nor the **Article 50(6)** interaction with Articles 13 and 50's exemptions (law-enforcement carve-out; the "obvious from the point of view of a reasonably well-informed... person" carve-out in 50(1)). Treat my paragraph numbering above as indicative.
- I could not verify whether the Digital Omnibus made **any** amendment to Article 50 beyond the reported watermarking transition. Sources are split in emphasis: Usercentrics reports a 4-month transition for the marking duty; aiactblog.nl and Goodwin frame Article 50 as wholly untouched. **This is a direct conflict on a load-bearing date and must be resolved against the OJ text.**
- I found no source addressing whether **real-time synthetic-voice output over a messaging channel** satisfies Article 50(2) machine-readable marking via audio watermarking, and what technical standard (if any) the Commission considers adequate. The harmonised standards were still reported as delayed as of Aug 2026 ([Plesner](https://plesner.com/en/news/ai-act-august-2026-what-expect-delayed-standards-pending-guidance-and-digital-omnibus-ai)).
- I found no source on **which national authority in Spain and France is the designated AI Act market surveillance authority** for Article 50 enforcement, or on the applicable penalty tier for Article 50 breaches (Article 99).

## Q2. Voice as biometric and personal data under GDPR

### Takeaway
The EDPB's position is that **voice data is inherently biometric personal data**, but Article 9 special-category status attaches only where the processing is **for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person** — i.e. speaker verification/voiceprinting, not plain speech-to-text. Where it does attach, explicit consent is effectively the only workable Article 9(2) basis, and the EDPB requires an **equivalent-functionality alternative that does not process a voiceprint** for that consent to be "freely given." Voice cloning is separately consent-gated. Article 22 reaches further than it looks after *SCHUFA*.

### Cited Findings

**Is voice biometric / Article 9 data?**
- **EDPB Guidelines 02/2021 on Virtual Voice Assistants (VVAs), v2.0 adopted July 2021**: "voice data is inherently biometric personal data." Voice is described as unique, permanent and intimate, and unlike a password cannot be changed. — [EDPB Guidelines 02/2021 on VVA, v2.0 adopted (PDF)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/edpb_guidelines_202102_on_vva_v2.0_adopted_en.pdf)
- Article 9 covers "the processing of […] biometric data **for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person**" — voice qualifies when used to that end. — [EDPB Guidelines 02/2021 on VVA](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/edpb_guidelines_202102_on_vva_v2.0_adopted_en.pdf); summarised at [Fox Rothschild — EDPB Issues Draft Guidelines on Data Protection Aspects of Voice Assistants](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2021/04/articles/european-union/gdpr/edpb-issues-draft-guidelines-on-data-protection-aspects-of-voice-assistants/)
- Consequence: processing a **voiceprint for identification requires explicit consent**; the EDPB specifies consent can only be obtained from **registered** users, and to be "freely given" the VVA **must offer an equivalent-functionality alternative that does not entail processing a voiceprint.** — [EDPB Guidelines 02/2021 on VVA](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/edpb_guidelines_202102_on_vva_v2.0_adopted_en.pdf); [Lexology — New EDPB guidelines on virtual voice assistants](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=9723c5e4-10c1-4f25-a540-a6702b01514d); [Pearl Cohen — EDPB Issues Draft Guidance on Virtual Voice Assistants](https://www.pearlcohen.com/edpb-issues-draft-guidance-on-virtual-voice-assistants/)
- Spain's AEPD adds an inference angle: **biometric analysis of the human voice can extract over one hundred distinct parameters**, from which health and physical/psychological conditions can be inferred — i.e. voice audio can leak Article 9 health data even where no voiceprint is created. — [AEPD, Guía sobre tratamientos de control de presencia mediante sistemas biométricos (PDF)](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-control-presencia-biometrico.pdf); [AEPD press note on the biometric guide](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/la-aepd-publica-una-guia-sobre-la-utilizacion-de-datos)

**Voice cloning**
- Under the AI Act framing, cloned voices fall in the Article 50 synthetic-content/deepfake transparency net from **2 August 2026**; separately, "a person's voice is biometric data and processing it (including for cloning) requires explicit consent under GDPR." — [SAC Studio — AI cloned voices: Belgian and European law (AI Act, GDPR, neighbouring rights)](https://www.sacstudio.be/en/blog/ai-voice-ethics/ai-cloned-voices-belgian-european-law-ai-act-gdpr); [Resemble.ai — The EU AI Act: What Generative AI Companies Need to Know in 2026](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/the-eu-ai-act-what-generative-ai-companies-need-to-know-in-2026)
- Cross-jurisdiction survey notes that France, Spain and others have taken **targeted** approaches — typically sexual deepfakes and election integrity — rather than a general identity-ownership right in voice. — [Harris Sliwoski — Deepfakes, Voice Cloning, and AI Impersonation: The Global Rules Are Already Here, and They Don't Agree](https://harris-sliwoski.com/blog/deepfakes-voice-cloning-and-ai-impersonation-the-global-rules-are-already-here-and-they-dont-agree/)

**Retention of recordings and transcripts (France, most concrete rule found)**
- CNIL limits retention of call recordings made for **quality control and continuous training** to **six months** from the recording date (active retention). — [Axialys — Enregistrement des appels & conformité RGPD: le guide 2026](https://blog.axialys.com/enregistrement-des-appels-rgpd-bonnes-pratiques/); [Leto — Enregistrement téléphonique RGPD](https://www.leto.legal/guides/enregistrement-telephonique-et-rgpd)
- Derived **analysis documents** may be kept up to **one year**; recordings with **evidentiary value** may be kept **five years**. — [Axialys](https://blog.axialys.com/enregistrement-des-appels-rgpd-bonnes-pratiques/); [LégiSocial — Écoute et enregistrements téléphoniques: quelle durée de conservation?](https://www.legisocial.fr/actualites-sociales/7880-ecoute-enregistrements-telephoniques-duree-conservation-donnees-personnelles.html)
- CNIL's older sector note **NS-57 ("Écoute et enregistrement des conversations téléphoniques")** remains the reference document for call recording. — [CNIL NS-57 (PDF)](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/atoms/files/ns57.pdf)
- CNIL published a **référentiel on retention periods** (HR-management context) dated **April 2026** which covers call-recording retention in the workplace. — [CNIL — Référentiel: les durées de conservation des données à caractère personnel (PDF, 2026-04)](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-04/referentiel_durees_de_conservation_gestion_des_ressources_humaines.pdf); [kohenavocats.fr (19 Sep 2026)](https://kohenavocats.fr/2026/09/19/ecoute-enregistrement-appels-telephoniques-travail-contester-effacement-cnil-2026/)

**Article 22 — automated decision-making**
- Article 22(1): a data subject "shall not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning him or her or **similarly significantly affects** him or her." — [Secure Privacy — GDPR Article 22 and Automated Decision-Making](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/gdpr-article-22-automated-decision-making-guide)
- **CJEU, Case C-634/21 (SCHUFA Holding), 7 December 2023**: an automated credit score is itself an Article 22 "decision" — not mere input data — because it plays a **determining role** in the downstream decision. — [TLT — SCHUFA case: CJEU rules on scope of Article 22](https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/schufa-case-cjeu-rules-on-scope-of-article-22); [A&O Shearman — CJEU rules that a credit score constitutes automated decision making under the GDPR](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-rules-that-a-credit-score-constitutes-automated-decision-making-under-the-gdpr); [Cloisters — CJEU's first ruling on Article 22 GDPR](https://www.cloisters.com/latest/cjeus-first-ruling-on-article-22-gdpr-credit-scoring-is-an-automated-decision)
- **Rubber-stamp human review does not break the chain**: a human who signs off an algorithmic output without genuine authority or capacity to reach a different conclusion does not make the process non-automated. — [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/gdpr-article-22-automated-decision-making-guide); [IAPP — Key takeaways from the CJEU's recent automated decision-making rulings](https://iapp.org/news/a/key-takeaways-from-the-cjeus-recent-automated-decision-making-rulings)
- Spain's AEPD explicitly links agentic AI to Article 22: controllers must revisit controller/processor allocation, legal basis and the record of processing, and **carry out a DPIA, especially where automated decisions under Article 22 exist.** — [Ecija — Agentes de IA y tratamiento de datos personales: las claves de la nueva Guía de la AEPD](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/agentes-de-ia-y-tratamiento-de-datos-personales-las-claves-de-la-nueva-guia-de-la-aepd/)

### Inferences
- The design lever is the **ASR/voiceprint split**. Speech-to-text for the purpose of understanding a travel request is ordinary personal data (with Article 9 risk from incidental health/disability disclosures in the *content*). The moment the stack does **speaker verification** to authenticate a returning customer, it crosses into Article 9 biometric processing and pulls in explicit consent + a non-biometric fallback path.
- Under *SCHUFA*, an agent that produces an output which in practice determines whether a customer gets a fare, a refund, or an EU261 claim outcome is exposed to Article 22 even if a human nominally presses "send." A genuine, documented human-override capability — not a queue of auto-approvals — is what the case law asks for.
- CNIL's 6-month quality-assurance retention figure is the only concrete number I found from a regulator; it is a defensible default for raw audio retention, with transcripts and derived analysis on a shorter or differently-justified clock. Note the 6-month figure sits in a **workplace call-recording** context and its transfer to a consumer-facing AI channel is an extrapolation, not a stated CNIL position.

### Gaps
- The EDPB VVA guidelines are from **2021** and predate LLM voice agents entirely. I found **no updated EDPB guidance specific to generative voice agents**. The EDPB's **Opinion 28/2024 on AI models and personal data** is the obvious adjacent instrument but did not surface in my searches and I could not fetch edpb.europa.eu pages to confirm its content or date.
- I found **no regulator-stated retention period for AI-agent transcripts** (as opposed to call recordings) in either Spain or France.
- I could not verify Spain's **Ley Orgánica 1/1982** (protection of honour, personal and family privacy, and own image) as it applies to voice; search returned nothing substantive. The extent to which Spanish law protects voice as an attribute of personality is **unresolved here**.
- No source found on whether **WhatsApp voice notes processed by an agent** are treated by regulators differently from telephone call recordings.

## Q3. What have AEPD (Spain) and CNIL (France) published on AI, voice assistants, call recording and chatbots?

### Takeaway
Both regulators are unusually active. **AEPD** has a biometrics guide, an "Adecuación al RGPD de tratamientos que incorporan IA" guide, an AI infographic, and — most relevant — **"Orientaciones sobre IA agéntica," published 1 February 2026 and formally presented 18 February 2026**. **CNIL** finalised its AI/GDPR recommendation series across 2024–2025, including a legitimate-interest recommendation, a 7-principle checklist, and — per secondary reporting — a position that **a voice AI agent must identify itself as such in the first seconds of a call.**

### Cited Findings

**AEPD (Spain)**
- **"Orientaciones sobre Inteligencia Artificial agéntica desde la perspectiva de protección de datos"** — published **1 February 2026**, formally presented **18 February 2026**. PDF at aepd.es. — [AEPD press note — La Agencia publica unas orientaciones sobre Inteligencia Artificial agéntica](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/la-agencia-publica-unas-orientaciones-sobre-inteligencia); [AEPD — Orientaciones IA agéntica (PDF, 76 pp.)](https://www.aepd.es/guias/orientaciones-ia-agentica.pdf); dates per [UCAM](https://iagenerativa.ucam.edu/la-aepd-publica-orientaciones-sobre-ia-generativa-y-agentes) and [Academia de IA](https://academiadeia.es/ia-agentica-proteccion-datos-aepd-publica-guia-2026/)

## Q6. Sector rules for travel (Spain, France, EU261, IATA/BSP)

### Takeaway
Both Spain and France require **registration/licensing to organise or sell package travel**, with **financial guarantee and professional liability insurance** as conditions. France's regime is national and concrete: registration with **Atout France**, guarantee typically via **APST**, under **Code du tourisme art. L.211-18**. Spain's regime is **regionalised** — the package-travel rules were transposed nationally by **RDL 23/2018** into the consumer code, but the *agencia de viajes* authorisation itself sits with the autonomous communities. I found **nothing** on the liability of an automated advisor giving fare or ticketing guidance — that is the single largest gap in this whole brief.

## Q7. Accessibility: does the European Accessibility Act reach a voice/messaging customer-service channel?

### Takeaway
Yes, on the sources found: the EAA had to be transposed by Member States by **28 June 2025**, and from that date new digital products and services — **explicitly including helplines and chatbots as support services** — must meet accessibility requirements built on the **WCAG POUR principles**. The requirement that most directly constrains a voice agent is that **where voice input is needed, at least one mode of operation must exist that does not require voice input.**

### Cited Findings
- The EAA had to be implemented through national legislation by **all 27 Member States by 28 June 2025**; from that date **all new digital products and services, and any updates to existing ones, must comply.** — [National Law Review — European Accessibility Act Compliance: What Businesses in the EU Market Need to Know (Jul 2025)](https://natlawreview.com/article/european-accessibility-act-compliance-what-businesses-eu-market-need-know); [Greenberg Traurig — same title](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/7/european-accessibility-act-compliance-what-businesses-in-the-eu-market-need-to-know)
- **Support services, including helplines and chatbots, must be accessible to users with disabilities**; standards derive from the **WCAG POUR principles** — perceivable, operable, understandable, robust. — [Master of Code — Reasons to Prioritize EU Accessibility Act Compliance for Your Chatbots](https://masterofcode.com/blog/eu-accessibility-act-for-chatbots-ai-solutions-guide); [Medium/Nerd For Tech — EU Accessibility Act for Chatbots and Global AI Solutions](https://medium.com/nerd-for-tech/eu-accessibility-act-for-chatbots-and-global-ai-solutions-ensure-compliance-before-the-deadline-0b51ab52315a)
- **Voice-specific rule**: "Where voice input is needed, **at least one mode of operation must exist that does not require voice input**." On a phone line, keypad entry is the established way to deliver that. — [Famulor — Is Your AI Phone Agent Accessible? The 2026 EAA Guide](https://www.famulor.io/blog/is-your-ai-phone-agent-accessible-the-2026-eaa-guide)
- Additional expected features for chatbots: **screen-reader compatibility, keyboard navigation, voice-control integration, captions/subtitles**. — [Master of Code](https://masterofcode.com/blog/eu-accessibility-act-for-chatbots-ai-solutions-guide); [Level Access — European Accessibility Act 2026: Requirements and Penalties](https://www.levelaccess.com/compliance-overview/european-accessibility-act-eaa/)
- General EAA readiness framing. — [LanguageWire — Are you ready for the European Accessibility Act (EAA) 2025?](https://languagewire.com/blog/are-you-ready-for-the-european-accessibility-act-eaa-2025); [KUDO — European Accessibility Act: How to Prepare for June 2025](https://kudo.ai/blog/european-accessibility-act-2025/)

### Inferences
- The no-voice-required-mode rule maps cleanly onto WhatsApp: because WhatsApp is natively a text channel, **a text path always exists alongside the voice path**, and the requirement is likely satisfied *provided the text path is functionally equivalent* — not a degraded fallback. The inverse also matters: a deaf or hard-of-hearing user must not be forced into a voice note, and a blind user must get text that a screen reader can handle.
- Practical consequence: **never make a voice note the sole carrier of information.** Every spoken reply should have a text equivalent in the same thread.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the **EAA's microenterprise exemption** (the Directive exempts microenterprises providing services — fewer than 10 persons and turnover/balance sheet ≤ €2m) or the **disproportionate-burden derogation**, which could materially change the analysis for a small operator. Not verified here.
- I could not confirm **Spanish and French transposition instruments** for the EAA, their exact national deadlines, or national enforcement bodies.
- **No source addresses whether WhatsApp itself, as the delivery platform, or the business operating on it, bears the EAA obligation** — a meaningful allocation question.
- The harmonised standard **EN 301 549** is the obvious technical reference but did not surface in my searches; its specific clauses for two-way voice communication and real-time text (RTT) are unverified here.

## Q8. Practical engineering implications

### Takeaway
The constraints converge on six concrete build items: a **first-turn AI disclosure** (satisfying both AI Act Art. 50(1) and Meta's first-message rule), **machine-readable marking of synthetic audio**, a **consent capture mechanism that works on a text-and-voice channel without dark patterns**, **tiered retention with separate clocks for audio, transcripts and derived analysis**, a **genuine human escalation path that can actually change the outcome**, and an **immutable audit trail**. A DPIA is triggered on several independent grounds.

### Cited Findings (mapped to the requirement that drives each)
- **Disclosure script — first interaction.** Article 50(1) requires the disclosure "no later than the first interaction or exposure," "clear, distinguishable and accessible." — [EC FAQ, Article 50](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act). Meta separately requires the AI statement "within the first message" from Jan 2026. — [Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/chatapp/use-cases/whatsapp-ai-policy-2026-guide). For voice, secondary reporting of CNIL says the agent must identify itself **in the first seconds**. — [Talkr.ai](https://www.talkr.ai/blog/securite-ethique/consentement-rgpd-agent-ia-vocal-appels-telephoniques-2026.html)
- **Synthetic-audio marking.** Article 50(2) requires outputs "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated" — watermarks or metadata. — [EC FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act); [nordflux](https://nordflux.de/en/insights/eu-ai-act-article-50-labelling-obligation-for-chatbots-and-voice-agents-from-2-august-2026)
- **Consent capture — recording/transcription.** If the call is recorded or transcribed the caller must be informed and the retention duration defined and limited. — [Talkr.ai](https://www.talkr.ai/blog/securite-ethique/consentement-rgpd-agent-ia-vocal-appels-telephoniques-2026.html); CNIL NS-57 — [PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/atoms/files/ns57.pdf)
- **Consent for any voiceprint.** Explicit consent, registered users only, **and an equivalent-functionality non-voiceprint alternative** for the consent to be freely given. — [EDPB Guidelines 02/2021 on VVA](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/edpb_guidelines_202102_on_vva_v2.0_adopted_en.pdf)
- **Retention policy — concrete numbers.** Raw recordings for quality/training: **6 months** active retention. Derived analysis documents: **up to 1 year**. Evidentiary recordings: **5 years**. — [Axialys](https://blog.axialys.com/enregistrement-des-appels-rgpd-bonnes-pratiques/); [LégiSocial](https://www.legisocial.fr/actualites-sociales/7880-ecoute-enregistrements-telephoniques-duree-conservation-donnees-personnelles.html); CNIL référentiel Apr 2026 — [PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2026-04/referentiel_durees_de_conservation_gestion_des_ressources_humaines.pdf)
- **Human escalation must be real.** A human who reviews an algorithmic output "but has no genuine authority or capacity to reach a different conclusion" does **not** make the process non-automated. — [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/gdpr-article-22-automated-decision-making-guide); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/key-takeaways-from-the-cjeus-recent-automated-decision-making-rulings)
- **Audit trail and agent-specific controls.** AEPD's agentic-AI guidance calls for **data-lifecycle traceability**, **memory compartmentalisation**, **meaningful human supervision**, technical isolation environments, strict update control, standard prompt-development procedures, and **active DPO participation** — and flags **difficulty tracing decisions** and **indirect data leaks when interacting with third parties** as core risks. — [AEPD — Orientaciones IA agéntica (PDF)](https://www.aepd.es/guias/orientaciones-ia-agentica.pdf); [Audidat](https://www.audidat.com/blog/noticias/aepd-orientaciones-ia-agentica-proteccion-datos/); [Ecija](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/agentes-de-ia-y-tratamiento-de-datos-personales-las-claves-de-la-nueva-guia-de-la-aepd/)
- **DPIA triggers.** (a) AEPD: biometric processing requires a **mandatory DPIA before processing begins**, documenting suitability, necessity and proportionality. — [AEPD biometric guide (PDF)](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-control-presencia-biometrico.pdf). (b) AEPD: introducing AI agents may change context, purposes, means and risks, requiring review of controller/processor roles, legal basis and the RoPA, "and where applicable a DPIA **especially if automated decisions exist under Article 22**." — [Ecija](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/agentes-de-ia-y-tratamiento-de-datos-personales-las-claves-de-la-nueva-guia-de-la-aepd/). (c) AEPD: agentic AI makes DPIAs "even more relevant" because it alters traditional data flows. — [Academia de IA](https://academiadeia.es/ia-agentica-proteccion-datos-aepd-publica-guia-2026/)
- **Accessibility.** Maintain at least one mode of operation that does not require voice input. — [Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/blog/is-your-ai-phone-agent-accessible-the-2026-eaa-guide)
- **Platform obligation stacking.** Two regimes now apply simultaneously to a Spanish-facing AI chatbot: GDPR (since 2018) and the AI Act (Article 50 transparency since 2 Aug 2026); the AI Act introduces "an obligation that GDPR did not have explicitly: informing users that they are interacting with an AI system." — [Levante](https://www.levanteapp.com/blog/chatbots-rgpd-2026)

### Inferences (engineering translation — these are mine, not sourced positions)
1. **Disclosure:** a single first-turn string, sent as text (not only as audio), covering (a) this is an AI agent, (b) the conversation may be recorded/transcribed and why, (c) how to reach a human, (d) link to the privacy notice. Sending it as text satisfies the screen-reader path; repeating it in the first seconds of any voice interaction satisfies the voice path. Re-assert the disclosure whenever a **new 24-hour window opens**, since a returning user after a gap is arguably a "first interaction."
2. **Synthetic-audio marking:** two layers — a perceptible signal (the disclosure) and a machine-readable one (audio watermark and/or metadata in the OGG container). Note the tension: WhatsApp transcodes media, so **container metadata may not survive delivery**; an in-signal watermark is the more robust bet. No source confirms what survives Meta's pipeline — test empirically.
3. **Consent capture on a voice channel:** do not attempt to capture Article 9 explicit consent by spoken "yes." Use the text channel for the consent act (button/list reply, logged with timestamp and message ID), reserving voice for the notice. This also avoids the dark-pattern risk and produces the artefact the audit trail needs.
4. **Retention tiers:** raw audio (shortest — CNIL's 6-month QA ceiling is an upper bound, not a target); transcripts (separate, shorter clock, since they are the working artefact); derived analysis/eval sets (≤1 year); anything retained as evidence of a booking or a claim (longer, on a contractual/legal-obligation basis, not on the QA basis). Voiceprints, if any, on their own clock with deletion on consent withdrawal.
5. **Human escalation:** an explicit, always-available "talk to a person" intent, plus **automatic** escalation on defined triggers (EU261 entitlement questions, refunds, any accessibility request, repeated failure, detected distress). The human must have authority to overrule the agent's answer, and that override must be logged — that is what takes the process out of Article 22(1).
6. **Audit trail:** per-turn record of model + version, system prompt version, retrieval sources, tool calls made, the disclosure having been sent, consent state, and any human override — retained on the same clock as the transcripts. This is what AEPD's "data-lifecycle traceability" and "difficulty tracing decisions" risk are asking for.
7. **DPIA:** treat it as **mandatory**, on at least three independent triggers — large-scale processing of consumer conversation data by a novel technology; potential Article 22 effects on fares/refunds; and biometric processing if any speaker verification exists. Do it **before** processing starts, per the AEPD's biometric guide.

### Gaps
- **No regulator has published a model disclosure script** for an AI voice agent in Spanish or French. Wording is unsourced.
- **No technical standard for audio watermarking** is identified as satisfying Article 50(2); harmonised standards were still delayed as of August 2026 ([Plesner](https://plesner.com/en/news/ai-act-august-2026-what-expect-delayed-standards-pending-guidance-and-digital-omnibus-ai)).
- No source addresses **consent capture mechanics on WhatsApp specifically** (whether an interactive-button reply is sufficient evidence of explicit consent under Article 9).
- No source gives **retention guidance for AI-agent transcripts as such**, nor for **vector/embedding stores** derived from conversations — a real and unaddressed problem, since deletion from a vector index is not the same operation as deletion from a transcript store.
