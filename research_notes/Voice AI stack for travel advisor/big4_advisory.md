# Big Four Advisory Positions on Enterprise Conversational and Voice AI Agents

> **Method and reliability caveat — read this first.** In this research environment the network egress proxy blocked direct fetches to `deloitte.com`, `pwc.com`, `ey.com`, `kpmg.com`, and also to `prnewswire.com` and `forbes.com`. Every attempt returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. I therefore **could not read any Big Four primary report, PDF or press release directly**. All findings below come from search-engine retrieval and synthesis over those same pages (the URLs cited are the canonical primary-source URLs the search engine surfaced and summarised) plus accessible third-party coverage. Figures should be treated as **high-confidence-but-unverified against the primary document**, and the report writer should re-check any number that carries real weight in the final report. Where I am relying on a secondary aggregator rather than the firm's own page, I say so inline.
>
> **Commercial-interest caveat.** All four firms sell AI strategy, implementation and assurance services. Every forecast, "maturity gap" statistic and governance recommendation below is published by a vendor of the remedy it prescribes. The pattern is consistent and worth naming explicitly in the final report: each firm publishes (a) a large market/adoption number that creates urgency, (b) a governance/maturity deficit statistic that creates a gap, and (c) a service line that closes the gap. Deloitte's "only one in five has a mature governance model", PwC's "fewer than half have rethought how work is structured", EY's "responsible AI governance correlates with better business outcomes" and KPMG's "only a third have cost visibility" are all of this shape.

## Q1. What has each firm published on agentic AI and voice/conversational agents, with names and dates?

### Takeaway
All four run a recurring, named survey franchise on enterprise AI — Deloitte's *State of AI in the Enterprise* (renamed from *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise*) and *TMT Predictions*; PwC's *AI Agent Survey*, *Responsible AI Survey* and *AI Business Predictions*; EY's *AI Pulse Survey* (wave-numbered) and *Responsible AI Pulse*; KPMG's *AI Quarterly Pulse Survey* (US and Global editions, plus sector cuts). **None of the four has a publicly identifiable flagship report dedicated to voice agents specifically** — voice/conversational work appears as a use-case slice inside the agentic-AI reports, chiefly under "customer support" / "customer service".

### Inferences
- The franchise naming itself is a signal: Deloitte dropping "Generative" from *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise* to become *State of AI in the Enterprise* (2026) marks the industry-wide pivot from model-centric to agent-centric framing. EY's Wave 3 subtitle ("a revolution stuck in an evolution") and KPMG's Q2 2026 framing (cost visibility, accountability) both indicate that by mid-2026 the Big Four narrative had moved from adoption-boosterism to unit-economics scepticism.
- Voice is treated as a delivery channel inside "customer service agents", not as a distinct research category. Anyone building a voice stack will not find a Big Four report that addresses latency, barge-in, ASR/TTS vendor selection or telephony — those are absent from the published corpus.

### Gaps
- **No Big Four flagship publication specifically on voice agents** (speech-to-speech, telephony, IVR replacement) was found. This is a genuine absence, not a search failure — multiple query formulations surfaced only vendor blogs.
- KPMG *Trust in AI* — could not confirm a 2025/2026 edition.
- EY *AI Pulse* Waves 1 and 2 — dates, sample sizes and headline findings not recovered.
- PwC *Pulse Survey* (the general US executive survey, as distinct from the AI Agent Survey) — could not reach the page; its AI-specific content is unverified.

## Q2. What numbers do they publish on adoption, pilot-to-production, ROI, and failure?

### Takeaway
The four firms publish **high adoption numbers alongside very low realised-ROI numbers**, and the gap between the two is the core of all four narratives. The single most consequential and most specific datapoint found is KPMG's Q2 2026 finding that **49% of organisations scaled back, narrowed, delayed or paused agent deployments once costs outran value, while only 7% reported established ROI** — a direct, quantified pilot-to-production failure rate.

### Cited Findings

**Adoption**
- PwC *AI Agent Survey*: **79%** of executives say AI agents are already being adopted in their companies; **35%** say adoption is broad; a further **17%** say agents are fully adopted across almost all workflows and functions — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html)
- PwC: **88%** of surveyed senior executives say their team or function plans to increase AI-related budgets in the next 12 months because of agentic AI; **over a quarter** plan increases of **26% or more** — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html); corroborated by [PwC Ireland press release 2025](https://www.pwc.ie/media-centre/press-releases/2025/ai-agent-survey-2025.html)
- PwC: **73%** agree that how they use AI agents will give them significant competitive advantage within 12 months — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html)
- KPMG *Global AI Pulse Q2 2026*: **32%** are deploying and scaling agents; a further **27%** are orchestrating multiple agents across the business — [KPMG International](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ai-and-technology/ai-pulse.html)
- KPMG US Q1 2026: overall agent deployment **54%**; among technology-sector leaders **80%** (~30pp higher) — [KPMG Technology Pulse Q1 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/kpmg-quarterly-ai-pulse-survey-technology.html)
- Deloitte *TMT Predictions 2026*: predicts **as many as 75% of companies may invest in agentic AI by end-2026** — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html) (**this is a Deloitte prediction, not a survey finding**)
- Deloitte *State of AI in the Enterprise 2026*: **58%** already using physical AI; **83%** view sovereign AI as important to strategic planning — [Deloitte US press room](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html)
- EY *Responsible AI Pulse*: **48%** of UK financial services firms currently using agentic AI, **+16%** planning within 6 months — [EY UK newsroom, Sept 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2025/09/ey-responsible-ai-pulse-survey)

**ROI and value**
- KPMG *Q2 2026 Global AI Pulse*: only **7%** reported established ROI — [ppc.land summary of KPMG Q2 2026](https://ppc.land/kpmg-finds-49-cut-ai-agent-rollouts-when-costs-outran-value/); primary [KPMG Global press release, June 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/06/growing-adoption-signals-progress-as-cost-visibility-and-accountability-drive-ai-value.html)
- KPMG Q2 2026: organisations with **full visibility into AI operating costs** report established ROI at **15%** vs **3%** for those without — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/kpmg-finds-49-cut-ai-agent-rollouts-when-costs-outran-value/)
- KPMG Q2 2026: planned AI investment held at a weighted average of **US$188m** over the next 12 months, flat vs **US$186m** in Q1 2026 — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/kpmg-finds-49-cut-ai-agent-rollouts-when-costs-outran-value/)
- PwC *AI Agent Survey*: **66%** of adopters say agents are delivering measurable value through increased productivity — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html)
- PwC *2025 Responsible AI Survey*: **60%** say responsible AI boosts ROI and efficiency; **55%** report improved customer experience and innovation — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/responsible-ai-survey.html)
- Deloitte *State of AI in the Enterprise 2026*: **66%** report productivity and efficiency gains as the top benefit achieved so far — [Deloitte US press room](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html)
- EY *Responsible AI Pulse* (Aug–Sept 2025): **81%** improved innovation, **79%** efficiency/productivity gains, but only **54%** revenue growth, **48%** cost savings, **56%** employee satisfaction — [EY Global, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/10/ey-survey-companies-advancing-responsible-ai-governance-linked-to-better-business-outcomes)
- PwC CEO data (2026 mid-year): about **39%** of CEOs told PwC their companies maintained or improved a positive AI impact — [LaunchReady.ai analysis of PwC](https://launchready.ai/insights/ai-readiness/pwc-ceo-mid-year-snapshot-ai-techno-resilience) (**second-hand; PwC page unreachable**)

**Why projects fail / stall**
- KPMG Q2 2026: **49%** had scaled back, narrowed, delayed or paused AI agent deployments after expected costs began to outweigh anticipated value — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/kpmg-finds-49-cut-ai-agent-rollouts-when-costs-outran-value/); also covered by [Forbes, 9 Aug 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/08/09/kpmg-says-nearly-half-of-executives-pulled-back-ai-agents-over-cost/) (Forbes page blocked; cited for corroboration of the headline only)
- KPMG: only about **one third** of organisations have full visibility into what their AI costs to run — [Enterprise DNA summary of KPMG Q2 2026](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/kpmg-ai-cost-visibility-enterprise-agents-q2-2026/)
- KPMG barriers to demonstrating ROI: **92%** skills gap, **78%** difficulty quantifying indirect/long-term benefits, **78%** difficulty scaling use cases — [KPMG US Pulse](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/ai-quarterly-pulse-survey.html)
- KPMG (technology sector leaders): **97%** cite data readiness and access, **82%** cite technical skill gaps, as top challenges to deploying AI agents — [KPMG Technology Pulse Q1 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2026/kpmg-quarterly-ai-pulse-survey-technology.html)
- PwC: **28%** of executives rank lack of trust as a top challenge to realising value from agents — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html)
- EY: **99%** of surveyed organisations reported financial losses from AI-related risks; **64%** suffered losses of **more than US$1m** — [EY Global, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/10/ey-survey-companies-advancing-responsible-ai-governance-linked-to-better-business-outcomes); corroborated by [Insurance Journal, 20 Oct 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2025/10/20/843969.htm)
- Deloitte *2026 Airline CEO Survey*: barriers to AI are **organisational rather than technical** — **more than half** cite lack of technical talent and skills, followed by organisational resistance/unreadiness — [Deloitte Insights, Airline CEO Survey](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/transportation/ceo-global-airline-survey.html)

### Inferences
- Reconciling PwC's "66% delivering measurable value" with KPMG's "7% established ROI" requires reading the question stems: PwC asks about *perceived productivity gain*, KPMG asks about *established (i.e. measured, booked) ROI*. **These are not contradictory findings; they are different bars.** The final report should be explicit about this, because naive comparison makes the firms look like they disagree when they are measuring different things.
- KPMG's cost-visibility correlation (15% vs 3%) is the closest thing any of the four publishes to a causal claim about *why* agent programmes fail: not model quality, but inability to price a unit of agent work. For a voice stack this maps directly onto per-minute cost of ASR + LLM + TTS, which is the dominant variable cost.
- No firm publishes a clean "pilot-to-production conversion rate" metric. KPMG's 49% pullback figure is the nearest proxy and should be labelled as such rather than as a conversion rate.

### Gaps
- No firm publishes an explicit pilot→production conversion percentage. Widely-circulated figures of that shape (e.g. "95% of pilots fail") come from MIT/other sources, not the Big Four; I did not find a Big Four equivalent and do not want to mis-attribute one.
- Deloitte's agentic-governance maturity figure ("only one in five companies has a mature model for governance of autonomous AI agents") appears in search synthesis of [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html) but I could not verify the exact wording, denominator or wave against the primary page.
- Per-channel ROI for customer-service or voice agents specifically: not published by any of the four as far as I could find.

## Q3. Operating model and governance recommendations

### Takeaway
The four converge on a common prescription — **ringfence what agents may do, keep a human escalation path, and govern agents as a registered population rather than as individual projects** — with EY the most specific on escalation and containment, Deloitte the most vocal that governance is lagging deployment, and KPMG uniquely emphasising **cost/token controls as a governance primitive**, which the other three largely omit.

### Cited Findings
- **Deloitte (firm recommendation/diagnosis):** agentic AI usage is poised to rise sharply over the next two years but oversight is lagging — **only one in five companies has a mature model for governance of autonomous AI agents** — [Deloitte, State of Generative AI in the Enterprise](https://www.deloitte.com/az/en/issues/generative-ai/state-of-generative-ai-in-enterprise.html); thesis piece [Agentic AI is scaling faster than guardrails](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html). The title is itself the recommendation: guardrails must be built ahead of scale.
- **EY (survey finding):** **80% globally (87% in Singapore)** have incident escalation procedures in place for when an AI agent behaves unexpectedly — [EY Singapore, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_sg/newsroom/2025/10/organizations-advancing-responsible-ai-governance-linked-to-better-business-outcomes)
- **EY (survey finding):** **87% globally (83% Singapore)** have policies that **ringfence what AI agents are allowed to do** — same source. This is the clearest published Big Four articulation of scoped agent permissions.
- **EY (survey finding + implicit recommendation):** **two-thirds of companies allow "citizen developers"** — employees independently creating or deploying AI agents — **yet only 60% of those provide formal organisation-wide policies and frameworks** to ensure responsible deployment — [EY UK, Sept 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2025/09/ey-responsible-ai-pulse-survey). EY's recommendation follows: shadow-agent sprawl needs a registry-and-policy layer.
- **EY (firm claim):** organisations advancing responsible AI governance are **linked to better business outcomes**, including winning more business deals — [EY Global, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/10/ey-survey-companies-advancing-responsible-ai-governance-linked-to-better-business-outcomes); third-party summary [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/ey-survey-links-responsible-ai-governance-to-winning-more-business-deals/). **This is a correlation presented by a firm that sells responsible-AI assurance.**
- **KPMG (survey finding):** only **36%** of organisations have implemented direct **token or usage controls** — "the mechanisms that actually let you intervene before a bill arrives" — [Enterprise DNA summary of KPMG Q2 2026](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/kpmg-ai-cost-visibility-enterprise-agents-q2-2026/)
- **KPMG (firm framing):** as organisations move from experimentation to deployment, focus is shifting **from deployment toward accountability, AI economics and value**; as multiple agents are orchestrated across workflows, the focus sharpens on **cost visibility, governance, and how work actually gets done** — [KPMG International Q2 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/ai-and-technology/ai-pulse.html)
- **PwC (survey finding framed as a gap):** only **45%** are actively rethinking operating models in light of AI agents, **42%** redesigning workflows, **44%** developing new agent-enabled products; **fewer than half** have rethought how work will be fundamentally structured — [PwC US AI Agent Survey](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html); corroborated by [Digital Commerce 360, 9 July 2025](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/07/09/ai-agents-us-business-pwc-survey-data/)
- **PwC (architecture position):** PwC advocates "**secure agentic architectures**" delivered through its agent OS — analyst write-up at [theCUBE Research](https://thecuberesearch.com/pwc-ai-agentic-enterprise-operations/)

### Inferences
- Mapping the four onto a governance stack: **EY owns containment and escalation** (ringfencing, incident procedures), **Deloitte owns the maturity/oversight narrative**, **KPMG owns FinOps-for-agents**, **PwC owns operating-model redesign**. A travel-sector voice deployment needs all four layers, and no single firm's published framework covers the whole stack.
- KPMG's token-control finding is the most actionable for a voice product, where cost per handled call is the make-or-break metric and where a runaway agent loop is both a cost event and a customer-experience event.
- EY's 87% ringfencing figure is high enough to suggest the practice is already normalised at large enterprises — meaning a voice-agent vendor selling into these buyers should expect scoped-permission and escalation-path requirements as table stakes in procurement, not as differentiators.

### Gaps
- **Agent registries, risk tiering, model-risk management (MRM/SR 11-7-style), observability, red-teaming and third-party model procurement:** I found no Big Four publication that names these explicitly in the material I could reach. EY's citizen-developer policy finding and KPMG's usage-control finding are the closest adjacents. The absence here is a limitation of blocked primary access, not evidence the firms are silent — Deloitte's and PwC's methodology pages very likely cover MRM and red-teaming, and the writer should attempt direct retrieval.
- Human-in-the-loop: no firm-specific HITL design guidance (when to escalate, confidence thresholds, handoff protocols) was recovered. EY's escalation-procedure statistic is the only proximate datapoint.

## Q4. Build-vs-buy for voice agents, vendor lock-in, multi-model strategy

### Takeaway
**I found no Big Four publication addressing build-vs-buy for voice agents, vendor lock-in, or multi-model strategy.** Searches on these terms returned only voice-AI vendor blogs. This is a real content gap in the Big Four corpus and should be reported as such rather than filled with vendor material misattributed to the firms.

### Cited Findings
- The only Big Four-adjacent signal on multi-vendor posture is behavioural rather than published: **Deloitte simultaneously runs alliances with NVIDIA, Google and Anthropic** (see Q6), and **PwC's agent OS is built with Salesforce, CrewAI and AWS** ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/deloitte-zora-ai-big-four-agentic-workforce/); [ChatFin](https://chatfin.ai/blog/big-4-ai-agents-ey-kpmg-deloitte-pwc-finance-teams-2026/)). Both are multi-vendor by construction.
- Deloitte's *TMT Predictions 2026* contains a structurally relevant claim: **agentic AI may begin to replace today's SaaS tools over time**, as SaaS apps become smarter, more personalised, adaptive and autonomous — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html). If read as buy-side guidance, it argues against deep commitment to incumbent SaaS-embedded agents.
- Deloitte's **sovereign AI** finding (**83% view sovereign AI as important to strategic planning** — [Deloitte US press room](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html)) is the nearest published proxy for enterprise concern about model/provider dependence, particularly in EU deployments.

### Inferences
- The generic build-vs-buy considerations circulating in the voice-AI market in 2026 — latency floors around sub-300ms forcing a custom co-located build, lock-in arising from proprietary connectors and closed abstractions rather than from the speech engine itself, and a hybrid "buy the platform, extend with custom" middle path — appear in vendor literature such as [Haptik](https://www.haptik.ai/blog/voice-ai-build-vs-buy), [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/ai-voice-agent-platforms-guide) and [Coval](https://www.coval.ai/blog/voice-ai-testing-build-vs-buy/). **None of this is Big Four material and it must not be attributed to them.** It is included here only so the writer knows the Big Four have ceded this question to vendors.

### Gaps

## Q7. Multilingual customer service and EU regulatory exposure

### Takeaway
**I found no Big Four publication on multilingual customer service or language coverage for AI agents.** On EU regulatory exposure, the authoritative material I could reach is from the European Commission and law firms, not the Big Four — but that material is directly load-bearing for any consumer-facing voice agent: **Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026** and require chatbots and virtual assistants to disclose that the user is interacting with AI.

### Cited Findings

**EU AI Act — Article 50 (primary and legal sources, not Big Four)**
- The European Commission published **draft Article 50 transparency guidelines on 8 May 2026** and **adopted final guidelines on 20 July 2026**, applying from **2 August 2026** — [Faegre Drinker, July 2026](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations); Commission pages: [guidelines library](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-ai-systems) and [policy page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations)
- Obligations cover four areas: **direct interaction with individuals; AI-generated content; emotion recognition and biometric categorisation; deepfakes and AI-generated text on public-interest matters** — [Greenberg Traurig, June 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act)
- **Providers of chatbots, virtual assistants and other systems intended to interact with people must design them so users are informed they are interacting with AI**; information must be **clear and distinguishable, at the latest at the time of first interaction**, and meet applicable **accessibility requirements** — [Greenberg Traurig, June 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act); [Article 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); practical guide [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/)
- Effective date and "is it a bot?" framing — [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/is-it-a-bot-eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-take-effect-2-august-2026/); scope and timeline — [Stibbe](https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-ai-acts-transparency-obligations-rules-scope-and-timeline)
- **An organisation with no high-risk AI may still have significant obligations under Article 50 purely because it operates a customer-facing chatbot**; transparency obligations are the **second most common compliance trigger after AI literacy, affecting around 33% of respondents** — [Greenberg Traurig, June 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act)
- **Penalties up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide annual turnover** for non-compliance — [Greenberg Traurig, June 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act)
- Emotion-recognition disclosure is in scope — relevant to voice agents that infer caller sentiment — [Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [high-level summary](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/high-level-summary/)

**Big Four proximate material**
- Deloitte: **83% view sovereign AI as important to strategic planning** — [Deloitte US press room](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html). The nearest published Big Four datapoint to EU data/jurisdiction exposure.
- EY's responsible-AI franchise is explicitly positioned around governance-as-commercial-advantage ("winning more business deals") — [EY Global, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/10/ey-survey-companies-advancing-responsible-ai-governance-linked-to-better-business-outcomes) — which is the closest thing to an EU-compliance-readiness sales argument among the four.

### Inferences
- For a travel voice agent operating in the EU, Article 50 is the binding near-term constraint and it lands **before** most high-risk-classification obligations: a voice agent must disclose its nature at first interaction, in a manner that works for an audio-only channel (i.e. a spoken disclosure, accessible, not buried in a pre-call ToS). Emotion-inference features add a second disclosure trigger.
- The fact that the transparency trigger catches organisations with **no high-risk AI at all** means a travel advisor deploying only a booking/service voice agent still carries obligations — a point the Big Four's general agentic-AI research does not make and which the report should source from the Commission guidelines directly.

### Gaps
- **Big Four EU AI Act readiness research/benchmarks:** not found in my searches. All four almost certainly publish EU AI Act readiness material (their regulatory practices are large), but their domains were unreachable and generic searches surfaced law firms instead.
- **Multilingual customer service:** no Big Four publication found on language coverage, translation quality, or multilingual agent governance. This appears to be a genuine white space in their published work.

## Q8. Where do the four firms disagree?

### Takeaway
Explicit, named disagreement between the firms is rare — they do not publish rebuttals of each other. The real divergence is in **framing and in what each treats as the binding constraint**, and there is one substantive tension worth reporting: **PwC's optimism about value realisation versus KPMG's evidence of cost-driven retrenchment.**

### Cited Findings
- **PwC (2025):** **66%** of agent adopters report measurable value through increased productivity, **73%** expect competitive advantage within 12 months, **88%** raising budgets — [PwC US](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-agent-survey.html)
- **KPMG (Q2 2026):** **49%** have scaled back, narrowed, delayed or paused agent deployments over costs; only **7%** report established ROI — [ppc.land summary of KPMG Q2 2026](https://ppc.land/kpmg-finds-49-cut-ai-agent-rollouts-when-costs-outran-value/); [KPMG Global press release, June 2026](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/media/press-releases/2026/06/growing-adoption-signals-progress-as-cost-visibility-and-accountability-drive-ai-value.html)
- **Deloitte (TMT Predictions 2026):** bullish market forecast — agentic AI market **$8.5bn in 2026 rising to $35bn by 2030**, potentially **up to 30% higher, as much as $45bn**, if enterprises orchestrate agents better — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html)
- **Deloitte (same report / press):** predicts **as many as 75% of companies may invest in agentic AI by end-2026** and that **agentic AI may begin to replace today's SaaS tools over time**; **inference will be two-thirds of all AI compute by 2026**, mostly in data centres on chips worth **over $200bn** rather than at the edge — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html); [BusinessWorld, 26 May 2026](https://bworldonline.com/spotlight/2026/05/26/752161/deloitte-2026-tmt-predictions-narrowing-the-gap-between-the-promise-of-ai-and-its-reality/)
- **Deloitte (State of Generative AI):** counter-weight within its own corpus — agentic AI "is not a silver bullet and all the broad challenges facing GenAI still apply" — [Deloitte Global](https://www.deloitte.com/az/en/issues/generative-ai/state-of-generative-ai-in-enterprise.html)
- **EY (Wave 3 AI Pulse):** framing in the title — agentic AI is "**a revolution stuck in an evolution**" — [EY PDF](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-us/insights/emerging-technologies/documents/ey-wave-3-ai-pulse-survey-full-report.pdf) (title only; PDF unreachable)

### Inferences
- **Substantive tension (PwC vs KPMG):** PwC's 2025 agent survey and KPMG's Q2 2026 pulse point in opposite directions on whether agent programmes are paying off. Part of this is timing (2025 vs mid-2026) and part is question design (perceived productivity vs established ROI). The honest reading is that **the market's mood shifted between the two fieldwork dates**, and that KPMG's later, more sceptical data should carry more weight for 2026 decision-making.
- **Framing divergence on the binding constraint:** Deloitte says *governance maturity*; PwC says *operating-model redesign*; EY says *responsible-AI practice and containment*; KPMG says *unit economics and cost visibility*. Each corresponds to that firm's strongest service line, which is itself the self-interest tell.
- **Deloitte is internally split** between its bullish TMT forecast and its own cautionary "not a silver bullet" / "scaling faster than guardrails" lines. Both come from Deloitte; the difference is audience (market forecast vs practitioner advice).
- **Common ground across all four (safe to report as consensus):** agent adoption is broad but shallow; governance and oversight lag deployment; the constraint is organisational (skills, data readiness, operating model) rather than model capability; and measured ROI is rare.

### Gaps
- No firm publicly contradicts another by name; all "disagreement" here is inferred from juxtaposed findings, and should be presented that way.
- Deloitte's $35bn/$45bn agentic market forecast could not be verified against the primary TMT Predictions document (blocked); it is corroborated by [BusinessWorld's 26 May 2026 write-up](https://bworldonline.com/spotlight/2026/05/26/752161/deloitte-2026-tmt-predictions-narrowing-the-gap-between-the-promise-of-ai-and-its-reality/), which appears to be sponsored/spotlight content and is therefore weak corroboration.
