## Q1. Which ASR products give the best measured WER for Spanish and French (not just English)?

### Inferences

## Q2. Which TTS products ship genuinely native-quality Spanish and French voices, incl. regional variants? One multilingual model vs per-language voices?

### Takeaway
The 2026 market has bifurcated: **single multilingual models that hold one voice identity across languages** (ElevenLabs Eleven v3 ~74 languages on one model ID, Inworld Realtime TTS-2 200+ languages from one clone, Cartesia Sonic for low latency) versus **per-locale voice catalogues** (Google Chirp 3 HD, Azure) that ship discrete regional voices (es-ES / es-MX / es-AR, fr-FR / fr-CA). For a travel agent needing peninsular vs LatAm Spanish and France vs Canadian French, the per-locale catalogues give explicit control; the multilingual models give consistency but regional control is implicit.

## Q3. How well does spoken language identification work, and what are its failure modes / production thresholds?

### Takeaway
Utterance-level LID is strong on clean, medium-length monolingual speech and **breaks down predictably on short segments (<~2 s), intra-sentential code-switching, accented speech and proper nouns**; production teams compensate with confidence thresholds (commonly ~0.6–0.85 for switching, 0.7 for STT fallback, up to 0.95 for auto-routing), debounce windows, minimum-word requirements and explicit confirmation prompts.

### Cited Findings
- Despite high accuracy on medium/long utterances, **LID performance on short code-switching utterances is "currently unsatisfactory"**; a BERT-based LID system was proposed specifically to improve short-duration code-switching segments — [BERT-LID, arXiv 2203.00328](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00328)
- **Below ~2.0 s of audio, language identification is highly likely to fail**; feature types have length-dependent usefulness — prosodic features work better on short utterances, phonotactic features on longer ones — [USPTO 12057102, language-identifying device](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12057102); [Language identification on code-switching utterances using multiple cues](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221481274_Language_identification_on_code-switching_utterances_using_multiple_cues)
- **Utterance-level LID fails completely on intra-sentential switches** — the utterance contains two languages and one label must win; the architecture receives input it was never trained to handle when a speaker switches mid-utterance — [Gladia: code-switching in speech recognition](https://www.gladia.io/blog/what-is-code-switching-in-speech-recognition); [Gladia: code-switching vs language identification](https://www.gladia.io/blog/code-switching-vs-language-identification-whats-the-difference)
- Frame/segment-level approaches: code-switching detection from **ASR-generated language posteriors** — [arXiv 1906.08003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08003); multi-modal transformers for utterance-level CS detection — [arXiv 2011.02132](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.02132); adapting language balance in code-switching speech (2025/26) — [arXiv 2510.18724](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18724v1)
- A 2026 paper **benchmarks commercial ASR systems on code-switching speech** (Arabic, Persian, German pairs) — the methodology is transferable to ES/EN, though ES/EN is not in that paper — [arXiv 2605.19069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19069)
- **Production thresholds reported by practitioner write-ups:** detection confidence drops below **0.6** with background noise above 40 dB, requiring threshold adjustment and confirmation prompts; require a **3-word minimum match plus confidence > 0.85 before switching language**, with a **500 ms debounce window**; **STT confidence < 0.7 triggers retry with a backup model**, with thresholds relaxed slightly for heavy-accent speakers; high-stakes domains route only when **>95% confident**, otherwise human-in-the-loop — [AlterSquare: multi-language voice agent architecture across 8 languages (May 2026)](https://altersquare.medium.com/multi-language-voice-agent-architecture-how-we-structure-prompts-and-fallbacks-across-8-languages-4be688ec2a7e); [futureagi: multilingual voice AI testing 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/multilingual-voice-ai-testing-2026/)
- Production guidance: require **explicit confirmation when LID confidence is below threshold**, use **multi-pass fallback** (a low-confidence detection triggers a second pass instead of committing), and **configure LID thresholds and fallback explicitly rather than trusting vendor defaults, which are tuned for demos** — [Deepgram multilingual STT production guide](https://deepgram.com/learn/multilingual-speech-to-text-guide); [AssemblyAI: multilingual voice agents](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/multilingual-voice-agent)
- Core challenges for multilingual voice agents (dialects, code-switching, per-language NLU) are catalogued by Rasa — [Rasa: challenges in building multilingual voice agents](https://rasa.com/blog/what-are-the-challenges-in-building-multilingual-voice-agents)

### Inferences
- For a travel agent, the worst LID case is exactly the common case: a short opening turn ("Hola, Madrid, el martes") mixing a greeting, a proper noun and a date. Sub-2-second first turns should not be trusted for language routing; prompt-and-confirm or let the caller pick.
- Proper nouns are structurally adversarial for LID: destination names ("Nice", "Lyon", "Cancún", "Málaga") are *in the other language* by construction, so a code-switch detector will fire on legitimate monolingual travel speech. Any switching policy needs an entity-aware suppression list.
- The threshold numbers found are practitioner anecdotes from blog posts, not measured operating points; they are usable as starting values, not as evidence.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or vendor-published **LID accuracy vs. utterance-duration curve for Spanish/French/English specifically** in 2026.
- No published ES/EN or FR/EN code-switching ASR benchmark with 2026 commercial systems (the only 2026 commercial CS benchmark found covers Arabic, Persian, German).
- No source gives measured false-switch rates at the thresholds quoted (0.85, 0.7, 0.95), so their cost/benefit is unquantified.

## Q4. State of real-time speech translation in 2026 (models, APIs, latency, quality)

### Takeaway
2026 is the year the "one API call for ASR+MT+TTS" product arrived: **OpenAI gpt-realtime-translate** (May 2026, 70+ input → 13 output languages, median ~711 ms to first translated audio), **Google gemini-3.5-live-translate-preview** (70+ languages, ~2.9 s on the same metric per one comparison), **Azure's speech-translation endpoint**, and **DeepL Voice-to-Voice** (April 2026, 30–40+ languages, voice preservation in 14). Research-side, IWSLT 2026 systems report ~27–42 BLEU at 1.7–2.3 s average latency on En→De/En→Zh.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For ES/FR/EN — all high-resource, all in every vendor's core set — the language-coverage differences between these APIs are irrelevant; **latency-to-first-audio and controllability are the real selection axes**.

- IWSLT numbers are En→De/En→Zh; En↔ES and En↔FR are easier pairs, so the 27–42 BLEU band is a pessimistic floor for a Spanish/French agent.

## Q5. Cascade (ASR → LLM translate → TTS) vs direct speech-to-speech

### Takeaway
The evidence in 2026 favours **cascade for many-to-many quality and controllability, direct S2S for latency and for into-English translation**. Most production voice agents still run cascaded; S2S is winning where latency dominates and the tool surface is shallow.

### Cited Findings
- **Into-English**: SeamlessM4T v2 scored **26.6 BLEU** on into-English tasks vs **22.0 BLEU** for cascaded baselines under 3B parameters — direct wins — [Deepgram: real-time S2ST architecture guide](https://deepgram.com/learn/real-time-speech-to-speech-translation)
- **Many-to-many**: cascaded **Whisper-LV3 + NLLB-3.3B scored 21.6 average BLEU across 27 language pairs** vs **15.8 for SeamlessM4T v2** — a ~6-point gap favouring cascade — [Deepgram S2ST guide](https://deepgram.com/learn/real-time-speech-to-speech-translation) (these are 2023/24-generation models; treat as **historical** for the direct-model side)
- **Latency**: end-to-end S2S is cited at ~200–300 ms response time vs ~2000 ms for cascaded pipelines in one benchmark framing (an **~85% latency reduction**), while production cascaded voice agents typically land at **1.5–3 s from end of user speech to start of agent audio** — [Gradium: cascaded vs S2S 2026](https://gradium.ai/content/cascaded-voice-agent-vs-speech-to-speech-2026); [futureagi: cascaded vs S2S 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/cascaded-voice-ai-vs-speech-to-speech-2026/)
- **Production reality**: most 2026 production voice agents still run cascaded; S2S is growing for short conversational use cases where latency dominates and the tool surface is shallow — [Coval: S2S vs cascaded, which to deploy](https://www.coval.ai/blog/speech-to-speech-vs-cascaded-voice-ai-which-architecture-should-you-deploy/); [Gradium](https://gradium.ai/content/cascaded-voice-agent-vs-speech-to-speech-2026)
- Research precedent for hybrid designs: direct simultaneous S2T translation **assisted by a synchronized streaming ASR** — [arXiv 2106.06636](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.06636); direct simultaneous S2ST with variational monotonic multihead attention — [arXiv 2110.08250](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08250)
- Modern single-call APIs (OpenAI gpt-realtime-translate, Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, Azure speech translation) **collapse ASR+MT+TTS into one call**, i.e. they externalise the cascade/direct choice — you lose the intermediate text unless the API exposes it — [LiveLingo guides](https://www.livelingo.io/guides/openai-live-translation); [Fora Soft real-time translation API guide 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/real-time-translation-video-call)

### Inferences
- **Controllability is the decisive argument for a travel agent**: a cascade gives you an intermediate text string where glossaries, do-not-translate spans, PNR/IATA validation and number formatting can be applied deterministically before TTS. A direct S2S model gives you no such seam — you can only prompt and hope, then verify post hoc from the output audio (or its transcript).
- Cost follows the same split: cascade costs 3 metered calls (ASR + LLM + TTS) but each is individually swappable and cacheable; S2S is one call, usually priced per audio minute, with no ability to substitute a cheaper component.
- The quality comparison in circulation rests on 2023/24-era models (SeamlessM4T v2, NLLB) and is likely stale against gpt-realtime-translate; the *direction* of the finding (cascade better for non-English-target, direct better into English) may not survive 2026 models. Flag as uncertain.

### Gaps
- No 2026-model replication of the cascade-vs-direct BLEU comparison (i.e. gpt-realtime-translate vs Whisper+frontier-LLM+TTS) was found.
- No cost-per-minute comparison across the two architectures with concrete 2026 list prices was retrieved.

## Q6. Keeping domain terms, codes and numbers intact through translation

### Takeaway
Two distinct problems: (a) **getting codes and numbers right out of ASR** — alphanumeric accuracy is far worse than word accuracy (best reported ~85% sequence accuracy on mixed alphanumerics vs ~98% on digits), and (b) **keeping them intact through MT** — handled by terminology-constrained decoding, placeholder/tag substitution, glossaries and post-editing. Text normalization per locale is reported as the single most common defect in production multilingual voice agents.

### Cited Findings
- **Alphanumeric accuracy (vendor-reported)**: Speechmatics reports **96.9% sequence accuracy on character strings, 98.0% on digits, and 85.4% on mixed alphanumerics** — the mixed case is exactly a record locator or fare basis — [Speechmatics: alphanumeric speech recognition](https://www.speechmatics.com/company/articles-and-news/alphanumeric-speech-recognition-why-voice-assistants-mangle-skus-and-how-to-fix-it)
- **Entity error is the metric nobody benchmarks**: one head-to-head reports a **16.7% missed-entity rate (~1 in 6 named entities)** for a competitor, and **15.31% entity error rate for Universal-3.5 Pro Realtime on agent-conversation audio**; guidance is to measure WER **on alphanumeric tokens specifically** (phone numbers, confirmation codes, emails, dollar amounts) because general WER is misleading — [AssemblyAI: the voice agent accuracy problem nobody benchmarks](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/voice-agent-accuracy-problem-benchmarks) (**vendor-run comparison — both the favourable and unfavourable numbers come from AssemblyAI**)
- **Structure priors help**: an airport code is a 3-character sequence with an expected alphabetic pattern; patents describe **alphanumeric sequence biasing via a rendered system prompt** to enforce such expectations at decode time — [USPTO 12283278](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12283278); record locators are auto-generated alphanumeric identifiers in airline reservation systems — [Wikipedia: record locator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_locator)
- **Keyterm prompting** is offered as a multilingual biasing mechanism by Deepgram Nova-3 — [Deepgram: Nova-3 + multilingual keyterm prompting](https://deepgram.com/learn/deepgram-expands-nova-3-with-10-new-languages-and-multilingual-keyterm-prompting)
- **Terminology-constrained MT** is a mature field: constrained decoding via **Grid Beam Search** guarantees given phrases appear in output; modern variants use finite-state automata, multi-stack/cascaded beam search, or attention-guided placement — [Neural MT decoding with terminology constraints, arXiv 1805.03750](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.03750); [ACL N18-2081](https://aclanthology.org/N18-2081.pdf); [EmergentMind: terminology-constrained MT overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/terminology-constrained-machine-translation)
- **Placeholder/tag substitution for do-not-translate spans**: entities are replaced with special tags that pass through translation unchanged and are re-substituted in post-processing using attention weights; correct for entities but requires entity-tagged training data — [EmergentMind overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/terminology-constrained-machine-translation); [Lingua Custodia at WMT'19, terminology control](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.04618)
- **Constrained decoding + LLM prompting combined** was the WMT 2023 terminology-task approach — [Terminology-aware translation with constrained decoding and LLM prompting, arXiv 2310.05824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05824); [WMT23 paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2023.wmt-1.80.pdf)
- **Post-hoc enforcement**: incorporating terminology constraints in **automatic post-editing** — [arXiv 2010.09608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09608)
- **Commercial MT glossary features have known limits** — a systematic study of glossary functionality across commercial MT engines documents where enforcement silently fails — [MT Summit 2021, glossary functionality in commercial MT](https://aclanthology.org/2021.mtsummit-up.8.pdf)
- **Morphologically rich targets are the hard case**: enforcing a glossary term verbatim in Spanish/French breaks agreement (number/gender), so lemma-based constraints are needed rather than surface-form constraints — [RWS Language Weaver Issue #154: terminology in MT for morphologically rich languages](https://www.rws.com/language-weaver/blog/Issue-154-Terminology-in-MT-for-Morphologically-Rich-Languages/); morphologically incorrect variants from failed number/gender agreement harm fluency and adequacy — [Translate With Care, ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.26.pdf)
- **Locale text normalization is the most-broken layer**: "1.234" is *one point two three four* in English but *one thousand two hundred thirty four* in Spanish/German; date order flips by region; acronyms need per-language expansion rules — described as **the single most common defect in production voice agents**, and normalization is **language-specific, not locale-specific** in many engines (en-US and en-GB normalized identically despite differing conventions) — [Gradium: making TTS pronounce numbers, dates, currencies, phone numbers](https://gradium.ai/content/how-to-make-tts-pronounce-numbers-dates-currencies-phone-numbers); [Inworld multilingual TTS docs](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual)
- Classical normalization machinery: **Kestrel** (Google's TTS text normalization) compiles per-language normalization grammars into **weighted finite-state transducers**, with French and Spanish among supported language aliases — [Kestrel, Natural Language Engineering](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/natural-language-engineering/article/abs/kestrel-tts-text-normalization-system/F0C18A3F596B75D83B75C479E23795DA); lighter-weight alternative for fast deployment — [Proteno, arXiv 2104.07777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07777)

### Inferences
- The defensible pattern for a travel agent is **extract-freeze-verify**: after ASR, extract candidate codes/numbers with format regexes (IATA `[A-Z]{3}`, PNR 6-char alphanumeric, ISO dates, currency amounts), validate against a live reference list (IATA/ICAO tables, the actual PNR in the GDS), replace them with placeholders before translation, re-substitute after, and re-verify the final string before TTS. This is the only approach where both the MT literature (placeholder substitution) and the production write-ups (normalization per locale) point the same way.
- A verbatim glossary is the *wrong* tool for anything inflected in Spanish/French (e.g. "boarding pass" → *tarjeta de embarque* must agree in number); reserve verbatim constraints for genuinely opaque tokens (codes, brand names) and use lemma-based or prompt-level guidance for domain vocabulary.
- 85.4% mixed-alphanumeric sequence accuracy means **roughly 1 in 7 spoken record locators is wrong on first pass** — read-back confirmation is not optional, and read-back must use the locale's own alphabet conventions (Spanish and French spelling alphabets differ from the English NATO one).

### Gaps
- No public benchmark of **IATA/PNR-specific recognition accuracy** in Spanish or French by any vendor.
- No 2026 measurement of glossary/do-not-translate enforcement rates for **LLM-based** translation (as opposed to NMT with constrained decoding); the constrained-decoding literature predates the LLM-translation shift.
- No source quantifies how often locale normalization defects actually occur in production (the "most common defect" claim is asserted, not measured).

## Q7. Evaluating and monitoring multilingual voice quality in production

### Takeaway
The 2026 stack is: **per-language WER targets with language-specific thresholds**, **entity/alphanumeric error rate as a first-class metric**, **COMET (+ chrF, XCOMET) rather than BLEU for translation**, **MOS/human native-speaker review for synthesis**, and **per-language drift monitoring** — the recurring warning is that regressions hide in cross-language aggregates.

### Cited Findings
- **Per-language thresholds, not one global target**: baseline performance then monitor drift by language over time; target WER **under 10% for English**, with adjusted thresholds per language (e.g. **under 15% Hindi, under 12% German**) — [Hamming: how to test multilingual voice agents](https://hamming.ai/resources/multilingual-voice-agent-testing)
- **"Regressions hide in aggregates"**: track recognition accuracy, containment, escalation and sentiment **by language**; ASR and NLU model updates can improve one language while degrading another, often without warning — [Hamming multilingual testing framework](https://hamming.ai/resources/multilingual-voice-agent-testing)
- **Treat every language as its own launch**: measure ASR accuracy across that language's accents and dialects including names and alphanumerics, evaluate synthesis naturalness **with native speakers**, repeat conversational tests (interruptions, repair, multi-intent) per language, validate localized knowledge and compliance wording — [Hamming](https://hamming.ai/resources/multilingual-voice-agent-testing)
- **Operational instrumentation**: track STT confidence per language, flag low-confidence transcriptions for human review, measure task completion and escalation rates by language — [Hamming](https://hamming.ai/resources/multilingual-voice-agent-testing); [futureagi multilingual voice AI testing 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/multilingual-voice-ai-testing-2026/)
- **QA tooling criteria (7-point framework)**: end-to-end testing with audio analysis; call simulation at 1,000+ concurrent calls; multilingual support for 10+ languages incl. dialect and code-switching; load testing at **P50/P90/P99 latency**; regression testing with **semantic drift detection**; root-cause traceability to audio and logs; automated reports and dashboards — [Hamming: evaluating voice agent QA software](https://hamming.ai/resources/7-non-negotiables-voice-agent-quality-assurance-software); [Hamming: voice agent testing guide 2026](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-testing-guide)
- **Metric definitions and benchmarks** for voice agents (WER, entity accuracy, latency percentiles, task success) — [Hamming: voice agent evaluation metrics guide](https://hamming.ai/resources/voice-agent-evaluation-metrics-guide); [Braintrust: how to evaluate voice agents](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles/how-to-evaluate-voice-agents)
- **Translation metrics**: neural metrics like **COMET correlate better with human judgment than BLEU** for LLM output; the recommended 2026 stack is **COMET plus LLM-as-judge fluency/adequacy rubrics with per-language-pair calibration** — [futureagi: evaluating LLM translation quality 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-translation-quality-2026/); [orq.ai: LLM evaluation metrics for MT](https://orq.ai/blog/llm-evaluation-metrics)
- **COMET has documented pitfalls** (version mismatch, score incomparability across variants, sensitivity to reference quality) — [Pitfalls and outlooks in using COMET, arXiv 2408.15366](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15366); the metric-development frontier as of WMT25 is **MetricX-25 / GemSpanEval** — [arXiv 2510.24707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.24707); WMT25 general MT system ranking — [arXiv 2508.14909](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14909)
- **Human-in-the-loop quality process**: **Errors Per Thousand (EPT)** — errors per 1,000 translated words from a linguistic QA process — as the industrial quality metric alongside automatic scores — [Translated: MT/LLM translation quality evaluation methods](https://translated.com/resources/mt-llm-translation-quality-evaluation-methods-setting-the-standard)
- **IWSLT 2026 evaluation convention** for speech translation: quality with **BLEU + chrF + XCOMET-XL**, latency with **LongYAAL** in both computation-aware and computation-unaware variants — [CUHKSZ IWSLT 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.13/)
- **TTS evaluation convention**: MOS on a 5-point naturalness scale (5 = "completely natural"), plus intelligibility measured as whether words are recoverable by ASR; recent work argues for phonology-informed evaluation beyond MOS — [arXiv 2509.04830](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.04830); [arXiv 2607.01965](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01965)
- **Agent drift is being formalized**: behavioural degradation in multi-agent LLM systems over extended interactions has a 2026 quantification paper — [arXiv 2601.04170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04170)

### Inferences
- A practical dashboard for this agent: per-language (ES-ES, ES-419, FR-FR, FR-CA, EN) rows × {WER, alphanumeric/entity error rate, LID confidence distribution and switch rate, TTS MOS from a sampled native-speaker panel, COMET on sampled translated turns, P50/P90/P99 latency, containment, escalation}. Aggregate views should be treated as decoration.
- **Language drift has two distinct meanings** that get conflated: (a) the agent answering in the wrong language / drifting mid-call, and (b) statistical drift of per-language quality after a model update. Both are monitored, but only (b) has a defined methodology in the sources found; (a) is best detected by running LID over the *agent's own output audio or text* and alerting on mismatch with the session language — no source found states this explicitly, so it is an inference.
- CMOS (comparative MOS) is the right instrument for "did the new voice regress?" A/B decisions, but I found no 2026 source describing CMOS use in production voice agents specifically.

### Gaps
- **No sourced description of A/B testing practice for multilingual voice agents** (traffic splits, guardrail metrics, stopping rules) was found.
- **No source explicitly describes automated language-drift (wrong-language-response) detection** in production; the term "drift" in the sources refers to quality regression over time, not language slippage.
- No CMOS-based production case study for Spanish or French voices.

## Q8. Known weaknesses of LLM translation for Spanish and French specifically

### Takeaway
The persistent, documented weaknesses for these two languages are **formality/register (tú–usted, tu–vous) applied uniformly rather than contextually**, **gender agreement and gender-neutral/inclusive forms**, **regional vocabulary and dialect**, and **numbers/dates spoken aloud** (a normalization rather than a translation failure). Machine translation in 2026 is assessed as strong enough to do more work but not to remove human judgment where nuance, compliance or brand voice carries cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Formality is a named evaluation dimension**: Spanish *usted* vs *tú*, German *Sie/du*, Japanese and Korean honorific levels must stay **consistent within a translation and match the source's social signal**, keyed to target locale, relationship and channel — [futureagi: evaluating LLM translation quality 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-translation-quality-2026/)
- **Observed LLM behaviour**: when translations are manually inspected for *tu* vs *usted*, models use *tu* almost exclusively for informal targets, while **formal translations are more varied and models often avoid second-person forms entirely** (an avoidance strategy, not a correct formal register) — [Improving machine translation formality with LLMs, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S154622182500150X)
- **Uniform-formality failure in tools**: translation tools often apply one formality level to everything — emails to executives read as casual as messages to colleagues, team updates read bureaucratically; "the words are technically correct but the tone is consistently wrong" — [futureagi 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-translation-quality-2026/)
- **Fixes**: LLMs used to revise NMT output for formal style, or LLMs as reward models scoring formality with RL to enforce it — [ScienceDirect: improving MT formality with LLMs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S154622182500150X); practical tu/vous guidance — [Pismo: English-to-French formality](https://pismo.ai/blog/english-to-french-formality-translation/)
- **Gender agreement**: Spanish nouns and adjectives must agree in gender; failures of number/gender agreement produce morphologically incorrect variants that harm fluency and adequacy — [Translate With Care, ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.26.pdf) / [arXiv 2506.00748](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00748)
- **Inclusive French is unreliable**: "current open LLMs are not reliable for inclusive English-to-French translation — without further intervention, inclusive forms are rarely produced, and **prompting alone does not close the gap**" — [Gender-neutral MT strategies in practice, arXiv 2506.15676](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15676); dedicated En–Fr gender-bias dataset — [FairTranslate](https://pith.science/paper/2504.15941); multilingual culture-first approach to misgendering in LLM apps — [arXiv 2503.20302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20302)
- **Regional Spanish**: register errors, regional vocabulary mismatches and gendered agreement failures are the three error classes MT QA tooling targets; 2026 work builds **LLM-assisted Spanish dialect corpora** to address regional variation — [ACL SIGUL 2026: LLM-assisted Spanish dialect corpus construction](https://aclanthology.org/2026.sigul-1.16/); [machinetranslation.com: English → Spanish (Spain), 22 models compared](https://www.machinetranslation.com/translation/english-spanish-spain)
- **Overall 2026 assessment**: MT is "strong enough to handle more work, but not sophisticated enough to remove the need for human judgment from enterprise localization"; generic LLMs "remain unreliable in content where nuance, compliance, or brand voice carries real cost" — [Translated: machine translation in 2026, an honest assessment](https://translated.com/resources/machine-translation-2026-assessment)
- **Non-English-centric pairs are worse**: across 15 models (incl. 4 reasoning LLMs) and 22 language pairs, **non-English-centric pairs consistently yield lower COMET scores than English-centric pairs** — relevant if the agent ever needs ES↔FR directly rather than via English — [Analyzing and improving cross-lingual knowledge transfer for MT, arXiv 2601.04036](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04036)
- **Numbers spoken aloud** are a normalization failure, not a translation one: decimal/thousands separators invert between English and Spanish/French, date order flips, and acronym expansion rules are per-language — [Gradium: TTS numbers, dates, currencies](https://gradium.ai/content/how-to-make-tts-pronounce-numbers-dates-currencies-phone-numbers); [Inworld multilingual docs](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/capabilities/multilingual)
- **Translation errors propagate into evaluation itself** — automatically translated benchmarks show result deviations attributable to translation error, so translated eval sets are themselves suspect — [Quantifying the impact of translation errors on multilingual LLM evaluation, arXiv 2605.24904](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24904); [Spanish and LLM benchmarks: is MMLU lost in translation?, arXiv 2406.17789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17789)

### Inferences
- For a travel advisor, the formality decision is a **product decision that must be pinned in the system prompt and verified in eval**, per locale: *usted* is the safe default for es-ES customer service, *tú* increasingly normal in parts of LatAm and for younger segments; *vous* is the safe default for fr-FR, with fr-CA tolerating *tu* earlier. The literature says models will not infer this reliably and will hedge by dropping second person entirely — which reads as stilted in both languages.
- Gender agreement failures interact directly with Q6: freezing a glossary term as a fixed surface string is a *cause* of agreement errors in Spanish and French. The two mitigations pull against each other and need to be designed together.
- Pivoting ES↔FR through English is likely to be the better-measured path given the non-English-centric COMET penalty, though it adds a hop of latency and a second chance to lose entities.

### Gaps
- **No quantified error rate** for tú/usted or tu/vous misuse by any named 2026 frontier model — the ScienceDirect finding is qualitative ("models often avoid second person").
- No measured comparison of **es-ES vs es-419 output quality** from the same model, nor **fr-FR vs fr-CA**.
- No travel-domain-specific MT error taxonomy or evaluation set was found for Spanish or French.
