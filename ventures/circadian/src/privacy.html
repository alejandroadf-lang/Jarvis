Circadian privacy

What the jet lag app keeps, where, and for how long. Last changed 28 September 2026.

In short: your trips stay on your phone. The server works out a plan and forgets it. It keeps something only when you ask for reminders or connect WHOOP, and you can remove either with one tap. Nothing is sold or used for advertising.

On your phone

  • The trips you enter, your usual sleep times and your latest plan, in the browser's own storage.
  • A random device ID the app makes the first time you open it. It is not linked to your name, email or phone number.

Clearing the site's data in your browser deletes all of it.

Making a plan

Your flight times, cities and sleep times are sent to the server to calculate the plan and sent back. The server does not store them. The "Add to calendar" link carries the trip inside the link itself, so nothing is stored for it either.

Looking up a flight

If you look up your flight by its number, the server sends the flight number and the date to AeroDataBox, which answers with that flight's airports and scheduled times. Nothing about you goes with it. The server keeps the answer for a few hours so the same flight is not asked for twice, and then forgets it.

Reminders

If you turn on reminders, the server keeps your device ID, your browser's push address, the trip's flights and your sleep times, and the times and wording of that trip's reminders, so it can send them when they are due. They are deleted when you turn reminders off, or when your browser tells the server the push address no longer works.

WHOOP

If you connect WHOOP, you sign in on WHOOP's own page; the app never sees your WHOOP password. The app asks only to read your sleep and recovery. The server keeps the access keys WHOOP issues and your WHOOP member number, against your device ID. Each time you open your progress, it reads your sleep and recovery for the dates of the trip, and the two weeks before it, to compare them with the plan, and does not store them.

With reminders on as well, WHOOP tells the server when it has scored your night, and the server sends you one morning check-in: last night against the plan, your recovery, and today's times. The server keeps only the date of each check-in it sent, so you get one per night.

Disconnecting WHOOP in the app revokes the app's access at WHOOP and deletes the keys from the server. You can also remove the app from your WHOOP account settings.

Usage counts

To learn whether the app is useful, the server may send counts of what people do to PostHog: a plan was made, reminders were turned on, WHOOP was connected, how bad jet lag was after a trip, and errors. These are tied to the random device ID and include things like "8 hours east", never your cities, dates or times. PostHog is not given your IP address. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, nothing is counted.

Who else gets data

  • WHOOP, when you connect it, as described above.
  • AeroDataBox, a flight number and date when you look up a flight.
  • Your browser's push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla), which delivers reminders.
  • PostHog, for the usage counts above.
  • Railway, which hosts the server.

Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used to build a profile of you.

Not medical advice

The plan is general guidance on sleep, light and caffeine. Talk to a doctor before taking melatonin or other supplements, especially if you are pregnant, take other medication or have a health condition.
