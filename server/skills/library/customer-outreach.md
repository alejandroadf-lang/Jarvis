# Emailing a real person

`send_customer_email` reaches an actual human being. It cannot be recalled,
and a bad one costs more than the reply it fails to get.

## Before sending

- **Check the contact notes first.** If this person has been emailed before,
  the previous exchange changes what you should say — and emailing someone
  twice with the same opener is worse than not emailing them.
- **Confirm they are on the allowlist.** Outside it the send is refused.
- **Know why this person.** If you cannot say what about them made them worth
  emailing, the email will say that too.

## What works

- **Short.** Four sentences. They are deciding whether to read it in about a
  second.
- **Specific to them in the first line.** Not "I noticed your company" —
  something that could only be said about them.
- **One clear thing you want**, and make it small. A reply, not a meeting.
- **Plain.** No "I hope this finds you well", no "reaching out", no "circling
  back". Write the way you would to someone you respect.

## What fails

- Talking about yourself before establishing why they should care.
- Claiming a result you cannot substantiate. A first customer can usually tell.
- Fake familiarity — pretending to have read something you have not.
- A paragraph of qualifications. They did not ask for a CV.

## The test

Read it back and ask: if a stranger sent me this, would I reply? If the honest
answer is no, do not send it. There is no cost to not sending an email, and a
real cost to sending a bad one.
